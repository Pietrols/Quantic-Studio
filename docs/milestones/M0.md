# Milestone M0: verified load flow from the command line

## 1. What works

- **Contracts v0** for networks, load-flow requests and results, diagnostics and provenance (WP-0.4), with a validating project loader (WP-0.5).
- **Two independent Newton-Raphson load flows** that share no solver code:
  - `qe_power.adapters.pandapower` (WP-0.6): converts a contract network into pandapower 3.5.6 and back.
  - `qe_power.textbook` (WP-0.7): a NumPy-only polar Newton-Raphson with an explicit Jacobian, written without reading the adapter.
- **Original fixtures** with recorded provenance (WP-0.8): a closed-form two-bus case and original three-, five- and fourteen-bus networks, plus `tests/fixtures/power/verify.py`, which rebuilds its own Y-bus to check any result for bus power mismatch and energy balance.
- **A command line and Markdown report** (WP-0.9): `uv run qe study run <project> --study loadflow --solver pandapower|textbook` writes `out/results.json` and `out/report.md`.
- **The M0 cross-check** (this WP): `tests/integration/test_m0.py` runs in CI.

## 5. Open items

These keep M0 open, or need a decision from Peter. None of them is hidden by a loosened tolerance.

1. **Fourteen-bus generator setpoints are not achievable within their reactive limits.** To hold 1.010 pu and 1.005 pu, generator-02 must supply 19.70 Mvar against a 1.0 Mvar limit, and generator-08 3.19 Mvar against 0.5 Mvar. The grid absorbs 21.74 Mvar. Both solvers agree; the report flags it. The fixture is **not** changed here (it belongs to WP-0.8). Proposal: see section 6.
2. **Resolved: shunt models now agree.** The textbook solver used to inject shunt power as constant power; PR #15 changed it to a constant admittance, y = (p_mw - j q_mvar) / base_mva on the Y-bus diagonal, as the contract requires ("power at nominal bus voltage", `docs/specs/network.md`). The sign follows from S = V conj(I) = conj(y)|V|^2, so a capacitor (negative q_mvar) gives positive susceptance. A new original fixture, `three_bus_shunt`, puts a 0.4 Mvar capacitor at bus-3, and this milestone test now covers it: both solvers agree on it to about 1e-14. Note on independence: PR #15 was written by Claude, the WP-0.6 author, at Peter's direction, without opening the pandapower adapter source; the M0 sign-off should check that change.

## 6. Proposed fourteen-bus setpoints (not applied)

Proposal: generator-02 at **0.9998 pu** and generator-08 at **0.9693 pu**, instead of 1.010 and 1.005 pu. Solved with the pandapower adapter on a scratch copy of the fixture, this gives generator-02 0.40 Mvar and generator-08 0.25 Mvar, both inside their limits. The grid supplies 0.23 Mvar, all bus voltages lie between 0.968 and 1.000 pu, and series losses fall from 0.1615 MW to 0.0044 MW.

Justification: bus-02 sits 2 km from the slack on a stiff 33 kV line. Its impedance is 0.5 + j0.76 ohm, which on the 10 MVA, 33 kV base (108.9 ohm) is only 0.0046 + j0.0070 pu. Across such a short reactance, voltage difference and reactive flow are tied by approximately dV = XQ/V. Holding bus-02 just 0.01 pu above the slack therefore forces about 0.01 / 0.007 = 1.4 pu, or 14 Mvar, through that one line, and the solved flow on line-01-02 is indeed 14.7 Mvar. The solved sensitivity is about 2 Mvar per 0.001 pu. On a network this stiff, a PV setpoint is really a reactive-power dispatch, so it should be chosen from the reactive output intended for the machine. Here that is roughly half of each limit: solve with each generator as a fixed P and Q, read the resulting bus voltage, and quote it to four decimals, because three decimals moves generator-02 by up to 1 Mvar. The 11 kV side then sits near 0.97 pu. Holding it closer to 1.0 pu would need more reactive power than generator-08's 0.5 Mvar allows, or a different transformer tap. That is a design choice for Peter, and should be a WP-0.8 fixture change rather than an M0 edit.

## 7. What we learned

- **Two independent solvers plus an independent checker is a strong test.** pandapower, the textbook solver and `verify.py` each build the admittance matrix their own way. When all three agree to 1e-14, the conversions are almost certainly right: line charging from microsiemens to nanofarads, transformer per unit to vk and vkr percent, the off-nominal tap on the HV side, and the sign conventions. A mistake in any one of them would show up as a disagreement far above rounding.
- **"Converged" is not "feasible".** The fourteen-bus case converges in three iterations to a mathematically exact answer that no real machine could deliver. Without Q-limit enforcement, a load flow returns whatever reactive power the setpoints demand. The report's back-calculated source table, checked against stated limits, is what caught it. Enforcing Q limits (PV to PQ switching) is a Phase 1 study.
- **Stiff networks make voltage setpoints fragile.** A 0.001 pu change moves about 2 Mvar. In real studies, reactive dispatch or power-factor targets are often a better input than a voltage setpoint on buses close to the source.
- **Circulating reactive power costs real losses.** About 0.16 MW of the fourteen-bus losses (7% of load) come from reactive power the sources push against each other. With sensible setpoints the losses are 0.0044 MW.
- **Small modelling choices matter even when today's tests pass.** Constant-power versus constant-admittance shunts give identical answers at 1 pu and different answers anywhere else. Writing the convention into the contract text and emitting a diagnostic made the difference visible before any fixture exercised it.
- **Write the convergence history down.** The textbook solver's mismatch history (2.3, 0.014, 1.1e-6 pu) is a quick sign of healthy Newton-Raphson: each error is roughly the square of the one before, scaled by a constant.
