# Milestone M0: verified load flow from the command line

Status: self-checked by Claude (the WP-0.6 and WP-0.9 implementer) on 2026-10-08. **Pending independent sign-off** by a different model (AGENTS.md section 4) and completion of the run metadata in section 3. Peter's supplied pandapower console output is now recorded there.

## 1. What works

- **Contracts v0** for networks, load-flow requests and results, diagnostics and provenance (WP-0.4), with a validating project loader (WP-0.5).
- **Two independent Newton-Raphson load flows** that share no solver code:
  - `qe_power.adapters.pandapower` (WP-0.6): converts a contract network into pandapower 3.5.6 and back.
  - `qe_power.textbook` (WP-0.7): a NumPy-only polar Newton-Raphson with an explicit Jacobian, written without reading the adapter.
- **Original fixtures** with recorded provenance (WP-0.8): a closed-form two-bus case and original three-, five- and fourteen-bus networks, plus `three_bus_shunt` (a capacitor bank on the three-bus case, added with the WP-0.7 shunt follow-up in PR #15), plus `tests/fixtures/power/verify.py`, which rebuilds its own Y-bus to check any result for bus power mismatch and energy balance.
- **A command line and Markdown report** (WP-0.9): `uv run qe study run <project> --study loadflow --solver pandapower|textbook` writes `out/results.json` and `out/report.md`.
- **The M0 cross-check** (this WP): `tests/integration/test_m0.py` runs in CI.

## 6. Fourteen-bus setpoint decision (applied 2026-10-08)

Decision by Codex under Peter's explicit authorization: adopt the proposed
**0.9998 pu for generator-02** and **0.9693 pu for generator-08**. Applied to both
`tests/fixtures/power/fourteen_bus_original/network.json` and
`examples/fourteen_bus/network.json`; the copies remain byte-identical.
Only these two voltage setpoints changed. Active dispatch, loads, transformer tap,
impedances and generator capability limits retain their authored values.

### Why these values

These are operating choices for an original synthetic fixture, not equipment data or
standard-derived limits. I checked the proposal with both solvers before selecting it.
It provides positive reactive support with substantial headroom while keeping every
bus inside the existing report's 0.95 to 1.05 pu band. Generator-02 retains about
0.601 Mvar below its upper limit; generator-08 retains about 0.248 Mvar. No limit was
expanded and neither solver enforced a Q cap to obtain these results.

The original 1.010/1.005 pu choices demanded 19.7046/3.1888 Mvar, far beyond the
1.0/0.5 Mvar upper bounds. Bus-02 is close to the slack through a short, stiff 33 kV
line, so a small voltage difference drives a large reactive exchange. The local
approximation dV ~= X Q / V explains the sensitivity; the full AC solutions, not
that approximation, decide feasibility. Keeping four decimal places avoids rounding
away a material part of the available reactive margin.

The lower bus-08 voltage accepts the existing 1.025 transformer tap and downstream
voltage drop. Raising it toward 1.0 pu would change the reactive requirement or need
a separate tap decision. The proposed values already give a useful feasible base
case without changing the topology or dispatch. They are not claimed to be a global
loss optimum or a guarantee for other loading scenarios.

## 7. What we learned

- **Two independent solvers plus an independent checker is a strong test.** pandapower, the textbook solver and `verify.py` each build the admittance matrix their own way. When all three agree to 1e-14, the conversions are almost certainly right: line charging from microsiemens to nanofarads, transformer per unit to vk and vkr percent, the off-nominal tap on the HV side, and the sign conventions. A mistake in any one of them would show up as a disagreement far above rounding.
- **"Converged" is not "feasible".** The old fourteen-bus operating point converged in three iterations while exceeding its authored generator capabilities. The revised point in section 6 satisfies those capabilities. Without Q-limit enforcement, a load flow returns whatever reactive power the setpoints demand. The report's back-calculated source table, checked against stated limits, is what caught it. Enforcing Q limits (PV to PQ switching) is a Phase 1 study.
- **Stiff networks make voltage setpoints fragile.** A 0.001 pu change moves about 2 Mvar. In real studies, reactive dispatch or power-factor targets are often a better input than a voltage setpoint on buses close to the source.
- **Circulating reactive power costs real losses.** About 0.16 MW of the fourteen-bus losses (7% of load) come from reactive power the sources push against each other. The applied setpoints reduce those losses to 0.0044 MW.
- **Small modelling choices matter even when today's tests pass.** Constant-power and constant-admittance shunts give identical answers at 1 pu and different answers anywhere else. Writing the convention into the contract text and emitting a diagnostic made the difference visible before any fixture exercised it. A dedicated fixture (`three_bus_shunt`) now exercises it.
- **Write the convergence history down.** The textbook solver's mismatch history (2.3, 0.014, 1.1e-6 pu) is a quick sign of healthy Newton-Raphson: each error is roughly the square of the one before, scaled by a constant.
